# Design Context — meetguns

> Voice, audience, and the argument the site is making. Read before any visual
> or copy change. The mechanical values — tokens, ids, durations, sync pairs —
> live in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) and
> [styles/press/tokens.css](styles/press/tokens.css). This file is _why_.

## What the site is

**The Schematic.** A living engineering drawing of the engineer.

One sheet, with registration ticks at the corners, a measuring edge down the
left, a title block at the foot, and six figures on it. Nothing is styled to
look technical. Things are actually drawn: exploded views, dimension brackets, a
detail callout under a real loupe, a parts list, a revision list.

The previous design was a printed press sheet, warm and hand-made. This one is
cooler, more exact, and it earns attention by being correct rather than by being
charming.

## Who is reading

- **Primary**: hiring managers and engineering leaders sizing up whether this
  person can build and lead. They have eight seconds and have read a hundred of
  these.
- **Secondary**: engineers and design engineers who arrived through the writing,
  microcharts, or a talk, and who will look at how the page is built.
- **Tertiary**: future collaborators deciding whether to write.

They skim. They came for proof. They stay if the page has a point of view and
does not waste their time.

## The argument

**Everything on this sheet is the real thing.**

That is the whole claim, and every figure is chosen to make it rather than to
illustrate it:

- Fig. 2's chart tray is the shipped `@microcharts/react` components, taking the
  live ink through the token bridge. A hand-drawn approximation would have
  quietly argued against the panel it sits in.
- Fig. 4 is `react-spectrum`, the actual npm package from 2019, running.
- Fig. 3 is sgb drawn as a general arrangement with its parts named, and it
  carries **no numbers at all**, because inventing a plausible-looking rate would
  have been the easy way to fill the space and the only dishonest thing on the
  page.
- Fig. 5 draws thirteen years as a dimension, because a career length is a
  measurement. The number is `CAREER_YEARS`, written once.
- The title block's clock and tenure count from the real first day rather than
  being typed as round numbers.

A reader who checks any one of these finds it holds. That is the point of doing
it this way, and it is why nothing here may be faked to look better.

## Three words

**Exact. Honest. Alive.**

- **Exact.** Line weight, never shadow. Zero radius on anything that is drawing
  geometry. Tabular figures. Numbers that are checked. A drawing has no rounded
  corners and no glow, and neither does this. The restraint is the credential.
- **Honest.** Nothing is a mock-up of itself. Where a thing can run, it runs;
  where a number cannot be verified, it is not written. Work done at Tracxn is
  described at the level of architecture, never with internal metrics, customer
  names, or anything from the source.
- **Alive.** A static drawing would be a portfolio of a drawing. This one has a
  light under the hand, a needle that points at the cursor, a traveller that
  rides the scroll a beat behind, a portrait made of halftone dots that shy away
  from the pointer. All of it responds; none of it performs. Nothing moves that
  the reader did not cause, apart from three one-shot draw-ins that are the
  drawing drawing itself.

The reader should feel: _this person is precise, and they are not overselling._

## The surfaces that carry it

When you make a design move, ask whether it supports these or fights them.

- **Six inks, two grounds, and no third axis.** Dust blue is the default because
  it is the ink a drawing office reproduced in: blueprint stock, the diazo line,
  the pencil-blue a draughtsman set out with. It is a material rather than a
  hue, which is the test all six have to pass. The other five are pigments a
  drawing office would actually have had, drafting amber among them as the
  colour of a hard pencil on tracing paper. Slate was retired for being dust
  blue at ten degrees and half the chroma: a second blue, not a sixth pigment.
  Olive replaced it because amber to bottle is the only gap wide enough for a
  sixth ink to sit clear of both its neighbours. Each has its own pitch, rising
  across the tray, so playing the picker left to right plays a run. The palette is a choice offered to the reader, not a brand statement
  shouted at them.
- **Warm paper and graphite.** The light ground warms as it darkens; the dark
  ground cools as it lightens. They are two materials, not one material with the
  lamp off.
- **The sweep.** Every palette change is carried by one WebGL band that passes
  across the sheet and lays the new state down underneath it. It replaced a
  circle opening from the control, which said "this control did it" but not what
  had happened. A roller passing over a sheet is the event. Left to right from a
  pointer, top to bottom from the keyboard, because a number key has no position
  on the page and saying so honestly is better than pretending. The band is a
  lit crest with a body, not a flat stripe: the straightedge is the drawing, and
  the roller is not part of the drawing. An ink pick sweeps its two colours; a
  paper change sweeps all six, because every ink is about to be repigmented.
- **The measuring edge.** A scale rule down the left with a traveller that lags
  the scroll on purpose. Tracking it exactly would read as a progress bar; this
  reads as a thing being carried along a rule. It reads its section names off the
  page rather than being configured.
- **The ask.** "Say hello" is a cell like the ones in the title block below it:
  one rule, the raised ground, and the words in the ink. It used to be the only
  filled area on the sheet, on the argument that the page's one request should
  be unmissable, and it read as a banner pasted onto a drawing. The ink marks
  what is live everywhere else here; it can mark this too.
- **The title block.** Not a footer dressed as one. Four cells and one contact
  row, two of them live. It earns its place by being short.
- **The sound.** A drawing-office cue set: a pen touching down, a switch thrown,
  a pitched note per ink. None of it is a notification. The whole site clicks
  like one instrument, and it is muteable from the header and from `m`.
- **The keyboard.** Hold Shift and the key glyphs float over the real controls,
  because the shortcuts are registered by the components that own them. `?`
  opens the help sheet. Power-user readable, never in the way.

## Copy rules

These are hard. They are not style preferences.

- **Zero em dashes and zero en dashes in visible copy.** Not in prose, not in
  labels, not in captions, not in metadata strings, not in alt text. Use a full
  stop, a comma, a colon, or the middot the drawing already uses as a separator.
  Every one was removed deliberately; do not let one back in.
- **Never the nickname "Guns".** The domain is meetguns.com and the handle is
  historical. In copy he is Ganapati V S, or "I".
- **First person, present tense.** "I am a full-stack engineer." Not "Ganapati
  is", not "we".
- **Short sentences. One idea each.** If a sentence has two clauses joined by a
  dash, it was two sentences.
- **Plain words.** No "leveraged", "spearheaded", "passionate", "cutting-edge",
  "seamless", "robust". Say what the thing does.
- **Every number verifiable.** 38 original public repos (194 including forks),
  2,649 stars, 16 npm packages, 106 chart types, 1.16 kB, 318 stars. These live
  once, in `PUBLIC_WORK` in `lib/resume.ts`, with the two API calls that check
  them in its comment; the repo count and star total are read live where a page
  can await them. A previous set said 55 repos and 15 packages, and neither was
  true. If you cannot verify it, do not write it.
- **No overclaims.** Not "the fastest", not "the best". The work is described at
  the size it actually is, and the least visible things are allowed to be the
  most useful ones.
- **Nothing internal from work.** Architecture is public-safe and is the
  interesting part anyway. No internal metrics, no customer names, nothing from
  the source. Facts about Tracxn come from its own published pages.

## What to avoid

- **A container inside a container.** The essay had its own ground and its own
  frame, on the argument that reading a thousand words through a drifting grid
  is miserable. That was true when there were three parallaxing background
  layers. There is one now, it is the dither, and it is five per cent ink under
  the cursor: the argument went with the layers, and what was left was prose in
  a box on a sheet. The sheet is the container. What keeps an essay readable is
  the measure, not a border.
- **Decoration that is not a drawing.** Gradients, glows, glass, blur, floating
  cards, drop shadows. A sheet of paper does not float. If a thing needs
  emphasis, give it a heavier rule.
  **One exception, and it is the only one:** the running header, once it sticks,
  is translucent and blurred. It is tinted with the ground rather than with
  white, separated by a rule rather than a shadow, and square. The read is a
  sheet of tracing paper laid over the drawing, which is a thing a drawing
  office actually does. Do not take this as licence for a second one.
- **Rounded corners on drawing geometry.** Panels, slabs, rules and axes are
  square. Two radii exist beyond that: a touched control, and a point.
- **Colour as decoration.** The ink marks what is live, what is an action, and
  what carries meaning. A wash is `--accent-soft`. Solid ink on a field label is
  wrong.
- **Harmonising this palette toward microcharts.** The sibling property's six
  accents are near-equiluminant because chart series must be. A drawing's inks
  must not be. The contrast between the two is what keeps them from being
  mistaken for one another.
- **Entrance choreography and scroll reveals.** Motion answers the reader. There
  are exactly three one-shot draw-ins, all of them the drawing drawing itself,
  and each is interruptible by touching it.
- **Faking a figure.** A screenshot where the component could run. A chart of
  invented numbers. A percentage that looks plausible. This is the one failure
  the whole design cannot survive.
- **A third palette axis.** Press runs were removed on purpose. Six inks and two
  grounds is already a lot of choice to hand a reader who came to find out
  whether to email.
- **Font drift.** Two faces, one job each: Hanken Grotesk for what is written,
  IBM Plex Mono for what is measured. Plus the Kannada cut, for the name. The
  reader can tell which class a thing is before reading it, and that only works
  if it is never violated.

## Editing this file

This file is read by `/impeccable`, `/typeset`, `/polish` and similar passes.
When a brand surface changes — an ink retired, a figure replaced, a rule
softened — update the relevant section here in the same change. Mechanical
values, ids and sync pairs belong in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) and in
[styles/press/tokens.css](styles/press/tokens.css); this file is for intent.
